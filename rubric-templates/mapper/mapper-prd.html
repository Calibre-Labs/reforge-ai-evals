AI PRD · prototype

Mapper

A market research agent for technology categories.

October 2026 · written before any traces
01

Product

You name a technology category or a company, and Mapper returns a market map: the top three companies, ranked, with the metrics you care about and the sources behind each number. When it works, you have a table you can paste into a doc in under a minute and defend in a meeting.

02

User and channel

A product manager, founder or investor getting ready for a meeting, in a web chat. This user will wait 20 or 30 seconds. They will not forgive a number that turns out to be wrong, stale or unsourced. Success is measured in figures you can defend.

03

Jobs to be done

  • Map a category I name: "top AI customer support startups".
  • Map the competitors of a company I name: "Reforge competitors".
  • Rank by the metric I care about, and always show the columns I always want.
  • Give me something I can share with my team.
04

Tool surface

ToolWhat it doesReversible?
get_preferences()Returns the user's saved preferences: the ranking metric and the columns to always include.Yes, read only
web_search(query)Returns titles, links and snippets.Yes, read only
read_page(url)Returns the text of a page, cut at a length limit.Yes, read only
build_market_map(...)Shows the ranked table in the chat.Yes, it can be rebuilt
publish_share_link(map)Creates a public link to a map. Anyone with the link can open it.No

One tool, publish_share_link, is the only action Mapper can't take back.

05

Autonomy

ActionAutonomyWhy
Reading preferences, searching, reading pagesActsRead only. No cost to being wrong.
Building a market mapConfirms scope firstA map of the wrong category or the wrong metric wastes the user's turn.
Publishing a share linkConfirms firstA public link can't be recalled once someone has it.
A request outside technology market research, or for investment adviceDeclinesNot the product.
06

Happy path

Thursday, 6:40pm, the evening before a board meeting. The user types "top AI customer support startups". Mapper reads the saved preferences (rank by revenue, always include customer count), confirms the scope in one line, searches, reads two or three pages, and builds a map of three companies with revenue, customer count and three sources. It adds one or two sentences on what stands out.

07

Non-goals

  • Markets where technology isn't the core of the product.
  • Investment advice. Mapper ranks companies. It does not say what to buy.
  • Private or proprietary data.
  • Long reports. The output is a table and two sentences.
08

Eval rubric

Success, in plain language: the map covers the companies, the metrics and the count the user asked for. Every figure has a time period and a source that supports it. Estimates are marked as estimates. A reader can check any number in under a minute by opening its source.

Known risks: a stale figure shown as current, an estimate shown as a reported number, a source that does not exist or does not say what the map says, a share link published when nobody asked for one, and a request outside technology answered anyway.

The rubric is its own page: Mapper eval rubric v1.

09

Release thresholds

MetricTargetWhy
Cost per sessionUnder $0.10Cheap enough to use before every meeting.
Time to first map, medianUnder 30 secondsA web chat user will wait that long for research.
Sessions failing any Governance lineUnder 1% at launchOne published link or one invented source is what people remember.

These are targets until there is data behind them.

10

Rollout

  • Internal use. The team reads every trace.
  • Cohort beta. A sample of sessions is reviewed against the rubric each week. Exit when the Governance threshold holds for two weeks.
  • General availability. The thresholds become the ongoing bar.
11

Open questions

  • What counts as "current"? The last twelve months, or the last fiscal year?
  • Should Mapper be able to change saved preferences, or only read them?
  • Which sources are good enough to cite for a private company’s revenue?
